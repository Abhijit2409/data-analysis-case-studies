=== 4b. ARE THE TIMESTAMPS CORRECT? ===
Test A - continuity across a spring clock change (turbine R80711, 2014-03-30)
  record before the gap (01:50): power   163.6 kW, wind 5.51 m/s
  the two sequences labelled 03:00-03:50:
    sequence A:  202.3  138.1  119.9   33.7   51.2   26.9
    sequence B:  172.6  207.4  247.4  259.4  241.9  254.2
  record after the gap (04:00):  power    34.2 kW, wind 3.81 m/s
Test A: sequence A runs continuously into the 04:00 record, and the other continues from 01:50. Both are genuine data, so the logger did not skip an hour in spring - the export relabelled one.

Test B - SCADA temperature against ERA5 reanalysis, by season
  winter (Nov-Feb)   n= 5751  best lag -1 h  (-3h:0.598  -2h:0.701  -1h:0.746  +0h:0.734  +1h:0.613  +2h:0.463  +3h:0.306)
  summer (May-Aug)   n= 5868  best lag -2 h  (-3h:0.837  -2h:0.887  -1h:0.870  +0h:0.794  +1h:0.647  +2h:0.464  +3h:0.259)

  Difference between seasons: -1 hour(s)
Test B: the best lag differs between winter and summer by -1 hour. The two tests together indicate the logger clock did NOT follow daylight saving, and the summer offset was applied afterwards. Summer records therefore sit ONE HOUR EARLY once converted to UTC.
Scope of that problem: roughly late March to late October each year, about seven months, not the 96 clock-change records. Wind and power share a row, so the power curve is unaffected. Anything that JOINS SCADA to another time series - reanalysis, air density, time-of-day - would be wrong by an hour for most of the year unless corrected.

turbine  missing_records  number_of_runs  longest_run_records  longest_run_hours  median_run_records  gaps_when_no_turbine_reported  gaps_unique_to_this_turbine

 R80790              450              16                  206               34.3                   8                            388                           62
Missing power values are clustered, not scattered: the longest single run is 132.8 hours. Long runs look like an outage or a turbine out of service, so treating a gap as zero production would invent downtime that the data does not show.
Most gaps are shared: 388 of them are moments when no turbine reported at all, which points to the data link rather than the turbines. Only [87, 821, 47, 62] gaps per turbine are unique to that turbine.

Meter vs sum of turbines, on 89,140 intervals where both are positive:
  ratio mean 0.9800   std 0.0083   5th pct 0.9718   95th pct 0.9883
  correlation: 0.999748
Meter-to-turbine-sum ratio averages 0.980 with a standard deviation of 0.008, and the two series correlate at 0.9997. This is consistent with the loader's statement that the meter series was derived from SCADA rather than measured independently.
Consequence: the meter series can be used as an internal consistency check only. It is NOT independent evidence of exported energy, so a meter reconciliation cannot validate the SCADA data here.

Cross-check on availability_kwh:
  intervals with availability_kwh > 0            : 3,842
  intervals with a turbine idle in usable wind   : 3,430
  overlap                                        : 3,074
availability_kwh is non-zero in 3,842 intervals, and 3,074 of those (80%) are intervals where a turbine was idle in usable wind. That close tracking is further evidence the field was derived from SCADA, not measured.
